Artaxias I Artaxiad A tier

Արտաշես Ա Արտաշեսյան

c. 190–160 BC

Why A tier: The founder of the Artaxiad (Արտաշեսյան) dynasty. He expanded Armenia from a small province into a major regional power, established a new capital, and enacted sweeping land reforms. His stewardship and martial achievements are foundational to Armenian history, even if his court lacked the complex intrigue or high culture of later eras.

  • 14After the Seleucid defeat by Rome at Magnesia (Մագնեսիայի ճակատամարտ, 190 BC), Artashes declared independence. He allied with Zariadres (Զարեհ), the ruler of Sophene (Ծոփք), to secure his western flank.

    He maintained a pragmatic peace with the expanding Roman Republic (Հռոմ) and the rising Parthian Empire (Պարթևաստան), avoiding devastating wars with superpowers.
  • 15Rapidly expanded his borders. He conquered Paytakaran (Փայտակարան) from Media, recovered Gugark (Գուգարք) from Iberia, and annexed lands from the Seleucids.

    According to Movses Khorenatsi, he decisively defeated an invasion by the Alans (Ալաններ). At the Battle of the Kur River (Կուր), he allegedly routed an Alan army, capturing their prince and securing the northern frontier.
  • 20A legendary builder and reformer. He founded the new capital of Artaxata (Արտաշատ) on the Ararat plain, allegedly with the advice of the exiled Carthaginian general Hannibal (Հաննիբալ).

    He erected boundary stones (արամեական արձանագրություններով սահմանաքարեր) across the country to regulate private landholding and protect village communities from encroaching nobles.
  • 48A conqueror and administrator, not a schemer. His reign is remembered for open warfare and public reforms rather than court assassinations or shadowy plots. While he established a Hellenistic court, his focus was on state-building, with little record of significant literary or philosophical patronage.

Tigranes II Artaxiad A tier

Տիգրան Բ Արտաշեսյան

95–55 BC

Why A tier: Many Armenians still treat him as the ultimate S-tier king because he built the largest empire in Armenian history. However, that empire collapsed within decades, crushed between Parthia (Պարթևաստան) and Rome (Հռոմ). His martial and stewardship achievements are legendary, but diplomatic miscalculations doomed the empire. He earns a solid A tier for his unmatched peak, even if his legacy was shorter-lived than others.

  • 10His diplomacy was bold but flawed. The Seleucid empire was exhausted by internal dynastic wars. To prevent total ruin, the Syrian (Ասորիք) nobility invited Tigranes to rule them in 84 BC. To secure his borders against Rome (Հռոմ) and Parthia (Պարթևաստան), he allied with Pontus (Պոնտոս).

    He bravely refused the Roman general Lucullus's (Լյուցիոս Լուկուլլոս) demand to surrender the defeated Pontic king. Yet, these moves backfired. The Armenian-Pontic alliance could not defeat the Rome-Parthia axis in the long run. Even his own son, Tigranes the Younger (Տիգրան Կրտսեր), rebelled at a critical moment. Nerve, but no lasting coalitions.
  • 19His battlefield record is nearly flawless. He conquered the Seventy Valleys (Յոթանասուն հովիտներ), seized Media (Մարաստան), and humbled Parthia (Պարթևաստան) to claim the title King of Kings (արքայից արքա).

    He won decisively at Damascus (Դամասկոս) and later shattered the Roman army at Aratsani (Արածանի). He did lose Tigranocerta (Տիգրանակերտ) when Lucullus outflanked him. However, his overall military peak remains unmatched in Armenian history.
  • 18He managed a massive, booming economy. Conquest multiplied Greater Armenia (Մեծ Հայք). He moved his court to Antioch (Անտիոք) to appease the Syrian elites. Realising the empire needed a capital on Armenian soil, he built the magnificent Tigranocerta (Տիգրանակերտ). (I once turned his capital-placement dilemma into a simple graph problem on SPOJ.)

    He controlled trade routes from Phoenicia (Փյունիկիա) to the east. He also boosted the economy by establishing "free cities" (ազատ քաղաքներ) with internal autonomy. He built for scale.
  • 10He successfully managed a vast multi-ethnic court, but was consistently outplayed in the shadows. Rome won the long game through subversion, and the gates of Tigranocerta were opened by traitors from within.
  • 12He brought Hellenistic culture to the elite. Movses Khorenatsi (Մովսես Խորենացի) notes his Greek (հունարեն) education. Plutarch (Պլուտարքոս) describes thriving Greek theatres in Artashat (Արտաշատ) and Tigranocerta.

    The court even tried to merge Armenian and Greek gods, equating Aramazd (Արամազդ) with Zeus (Զևս) and Anahit (Անահիտ) with Artemis (Արտեմիս). However, this was purely a court fashion. The common people rejected it, limiting his cultural impact.

Artavasdes II Artaxiad A tier

Արտավազդ Բ Արտաշեսյան

55–34 BC

Why A tier: A playwright-king who navigated the deadly rivalry between Rome (Հռոմ) and Parthia (Պարթևաստան). He maintained Armenian independence for two decades through shifting alliances, fielding substantial armies, and fostering Hellenistic culture. Exceptional learning and solid diplomacy, dragged down by military caution and his tragic betrayal by Mark Antony.

  • 14Navigated the fallout of the First Triumvirate. After Crassus's defeat in 53 BC, he allied with Parthian king Orodes II (Որոդես Բ), sealing the pact by marrying his sister to the Parthian crown prince Pacorus (Բակուր).

    When Mark Antony (Մարկոս Անտոնիոս) invaded in 36 BC with ~100,000 troops, Artavasdes shifted back to Rome to spare Armenia from destruction.
  • 11Fielded a formidable army, but was highly cautious. During Antony's 36 BC Parthian campaign, Artavasdes provided 6,000 cavalry and 7,000 infantry.

    However, when Antony's siege engines were destroyed at Phraaspa (Հրահատապատ), Artavasdes pragmatically withdrew his forces to preserve them. This earned him Antony's lasting enmity. He avoided pitched battles when possible.
  • 13Maintained the realm's prosperity and borders intact through a highly volatile era. He is not known for founding new cities or major economic reforms like his father Tigranes.
  • 15Survived for 20 years by reading the shifting balance of power between two empires. However, his final miscalculation was fatal.

    In 34 BC, Antony lured him to the Roman camp near Artashat (Արտաշատ) under the guise of discussing a marriage alliance. Artavasdes was arrested, paraded in golden chains in Alexandria (Ալեքսանդրիա), and eventually beheaded by Cleopatra (Կլեոպատրա) in 31 BC.
  • 18Plutarch (Պլուտարքոս) records that Artavasdes composed tragedies, orations, and histories in Greek (հունարեն), some of which survived into the 1st century AD.

    He famously staged Euripides's The Bacchae (Բաքոսուհիներ) at the Armenian court in Artashat, during which the severed head of the Roman general Crassus (Կրասոս) was reportedly used as a stage prop.

Tiridates I Arsacid B tier

Տրդատ Ա Արշակունի

63–88 AD

Why B tier: The founder of the Arsacid (Արշակունի) dynasty in Armenia. He secured his throne through a mix of Parthian military backing and a masterful diplomatic compromise with Rome (Հռոմ). A diplomatic masterpiece culminating in his journey to Rome, though his reign lacked the massive conquests or building projects of the Artaxiads.

  • 16Mastered the art of compromise. The Treaty of Rhandeia (Հռանդեայի պայմանագիր, 64 AD) ended decades of Roman-Parthian war. The agreement was brilliant: Tiridates, a Parthian prince, would rule Armenia, but receive his crown from the Roman Emperor.

    In 66 AD, he travelled overland to Rome with 3,000 retainers. Nero (Ներոն) crowned him in a spectacular ceremony, securing peace for 50 years.
  • 13Fought a grueling war against the Roman general Corbulo (Կորբուլոն). In 58 AD, Corbulo invaded with ~30,000 troops, capturing Artashat (Արտաշատ) and Tigranocerta (Տիգրանակերտ). Tiridates waged a guerrilla war.

    In 62 AD, with Parthian reinforcements from his brother Vologases I (Վաղարշ Ա), Tiridates surrounded and humiliated the Roman army under Paetus at Rhandeia (Հռանդեա), forcing them to surrender and pass under the yoke.
  • 13Rebuilt the capital Artashat (Արտաշատ), which had been burned by Corbulo. Nero provided him with 50 million sesterces and Roman artisans to aid in the reconstruction. He established the Arsacid dynasty that would rule Armenia for nearly four centuries.
  • 12Navigated the complex factional politics of the Parthian court and the Roman Senate. He successfully played the two superpowers against each other to secure his own position without falling victim to internal assassinations.
  • 14Tiridates was a Zoroastrian high priest, or Magus (Մոգ). Pliny the Elder notes that he introduced Magian banquets and rituals to Nero.

    His strict adherence to Zoroastrian purity laws (which forbade him from traveling by sea to avoid defiling the water) dictated his massive, nine-month overland journey to Rome.

Tiridates III Arsacid B tier

Տրդատ Գ Արշակունի

c. 298–330 AD

Why B tier: The king who made Armenia the first Christian state (traditionally 301 AD). He reclaimed his throne with Roman steel and fundamentally transformed Armenian civilization. His civilisational impact is unmatched, but his record reflects a pious, physically imposing warrior-king rather than a universal genius.

  • 14Restored to the throne in 298 AD following the Peace of Nisibis (Մծբինի պայմանագիր) between Rome and Persia. He maintained a strong alliance with Rome (Հռոմ) to protect against Sassanid (Սասանյան) Persia.

    His conversion to Christianity by Gregory the Illuminator (Գրիգոր Լուսավորիչ) aligned Armenia culturally with the Christianizing Roman Empire, permanently separating it from Zoroastrian Persia.
  • 16Famed for his legendary physical strength. Agathangelos (Ագաթանգեղոս) and Movses Khorenatsi (Մովսես Խորենացի) describe him fighting in the Roman army before his restoration, allegedly stopping chariots with his bare hands and defeating Gothic champions in single combat.

    As king, he successfully defended Armenia's borders against Sassanid incursions and northern nomadic raids.
  • 14Executed a massive cultural and institutional overhaul. He destroyed the ancient pagan temples (such as the temple of Anahit in Erez) and transferred their vast estates, wealth, and lands to the newly established Armenian Apostolic Church. This fundamentally restructured the Armenian feudal economy.
  • 11His reign was defined by open religious conflict rather than court scheming. He brutally persecuted Christians (including the martyrdom of the Hripsimian virgins / Հռիփսիմյան կույսեր) before his own conversion. Later, he faced open rebellion from pagan nakharars (նախարարներ) rather than shadowy plots.
  • 13While not a scholar himself, his reign marks the beginning of Armenian Christian literature and historiography. He patronised the early Church, establishing schools to teach Greek and Syriac to the children of pagan priests to prepare them for Christian clergy.

Khosrov II Kotak Arsacid C tier

Խոսրով Բ Կոտակ Արշակունի

330–339 AD

Why C tier: Son of Tiridates III who inherited a newly Christian Armenia and ruled briefly before Persian intrigue removed him — one of the thinnest biographies on the list, and Roman sources often confuse him with his grandfather Khosrov I. Total 55 — included because the Arsacid succession matters, but the evidence barely supports a higher score on any axis.

  • 12Persian pressure and court plotting ended the reign; the one axis where the sources hint at a story.
  • 12101110Nine years on the throne with almost no independent record in Pavstos Buzand or Movses Khorenatsi — a placeholder king between two giants, not a builder, conqueror, or scholar.

Arshak II Arsacid C tier

Արշակ Բ Արշակունի

c. 350–368 AD

Why C tier: A defiant king who waged a brutal, decades-long war of survival against Sassanid Persia. He tried to centralise power but alienated his own nobility and the Church. Heroic martial resistance, but his reign ended in diplomatic catastrophe, civil war, and his tragic death in Persian captivity.

  • 16Waged a relentless defensive war against Shapur II (Շապուհ Բ). Pavstos Buzand (Փավստոս Բուզանդ) details numerous battles where Arshak and his legendary sparapet Vasak Mamikonian (Վասակ Մամիկոնյան) repelled massive Persian invasions.

    In one notable engagement, the Armenian cavalry allegedly routed a Persian army of tens of thousands, though exact numbers in Buzand are often stylized. His survival against a superpower defines his martial legacy.
  • 13His court was a viper's nest. He ordered the assassination of his own nephew Gnel (Գնել) and his rebellious cousin Tiridates. His queen, Olympias, was poisoned by his other wife, Pharantzem (Փառանձեմ), who mixed poison into the communion wine.

    Ultimately, Arshak was lured to a "peace conference" by Shapur II, captured, and imprisoned in the Castle of Oblivion (Անհուշ բերդ), where he committed suicide.
  • 101110Diplomatically, he was abandoned by Rome (Հռոմ) after the "Ignoble Peace" (Ամոթալի դաշնագիր) in 363 AD, leaving him to face Persia alone. In stewardship, he tried to break the feudal lords (nakharars) by founding the asylum city of Arshakavan (Արշակավան). The lords retaliated by slaughtering the city's inhabitants (allegedly 20,000 families). He also clashed bitterly with the Church, leaving no scholarly legacy.

Pap Arsacid B tier

Պապ Արշակունի

370–374 AD

Why B tier: A pragmatic and ruthless restorer who clawed back Armenian autonomy after devastating Persian invasions. He curbed the power of the Church and rebuilt the army. Highly effective state-building and intrigue, but his reign was cut short by Roman assassination before he could fully stabilize the realm.

  • 12Reclaimed the throne in 370 AD with the help of the Roman Emperor Valens. However, Pap quickly realized Rome intended to treat Armenia as a vassal rather than an ally.

    He secretly negotiated with Shapur II (Շապուհ Բ) of Persia to secure true independence, playing the two empires against each other until Rome decided he was too dangerous.
  • 13Inherited a ruined kingdom. Alongside sparapet Mushegh Mamikonian (Մուշեղ Մամիկոնյան), he rebuilt the Armenian army to 90,000 men.

    In 371 AD, at the Battle of Bagrevand (Բագրևանդի ճակատամարտ), the combined Armenian-Roman forces decisively crushed a massive Persian invasion force, securing Armenia's borders.
  • 12Enacted radical economic reforms to strengthen the crown. He confiscated vast estates from the Church, closed down numerous monasteries and convents, and forced monks to serve in the army or pay taxes. This replenished the royal treasury and allowed him to rebuild the nation's military and infrastructure.
  • 16A master of ruthless politics. When Catholicos Nerses the Great opposed his reforms, Pap allegedly had him poisoned at a royal banquet. He navigated constant assassination plots from disgruntled nakharars and Roman spies.

    However, he was ultimately outmaneuvered: in 374 AD, the Roman general Trajanus invited him to a feast and had him hacked to death by guards.
  • 9Vilified by Armenian clerical historians (like Pavstos Buzand) for his anti-Church policies and alleged debauchery. He shut down religious institutions and showed no interest in theological or scholarly patronage, earning him the lowest learning score among the major kings.

Vramshapuh Arsacid C tier

Վրամշապուհ Արշակունի

389–417 AD

Why C tier: Peaceful king whose reign is remembered for one civilisational event: Mesrop Mashtots created the Armenian alphabet (c. 405) with his patronage. Total 61 — immortal through Mashtots, but personally a mild steward rather than a powerhouse ruler.

  • 13Kept Armenia stable between Rome and Persia.
  • 9A reign of peace, not campaigns.
  • 13Patronage that enabled the alphabet — indirect but lasting.
  • 8Quiet court; little scheming recorded.
  • 18Alphabet patronage is the highest Learning event in Armenian history.

Ashot I Bagratid B tier

Աշոտ Ա Բագրատունի

884–890

Why B tier: Restored the Armenian crown after centuries of foreign rule, recognised as king by both the Abbasid Caliph and Byzantine Emperor — the Bagratid restoration begins here. Total 67 — indispensable restorer, but a short reign limits the score ceiling.

  • 16Dual recognition from Caliph and Emperor is exceptional.
  • 14Fought his way back to kingship after Arab domination.
  • 14Re-founded royal authority and dynastic legitimacy.
  • 11More restorer than plotter.
  • 12Standard medieval royal patronage.

Smbat I Bagratid C tier

Սմբատ Ա Բագրատունի

890–914

Why C tier: Expanded the realm against the Sajids but was captured, tortured, and hung from the walls of Dvin — the Martyr-king of Catholicos Yovhannes’s eyewitness account. Total 57 — memorable death, middling reign.

  • 13Managed Caliphal politics until the Sajid crisis.
  • 12Won battles, then lost the war catastrophically.
  • 12Territorial gains undone by his capture.
  • 9Outmanoeuvred by Emir Yusuf.
  • 11No major cultural legacy beyond martyrdom.

Ashot II Bagratid C tier

Աշոտ Բ Բագրատունի

914–929

Why C tier: Drove out the Sajid armies in a brutal counter-offensive and was recognised as Shahanshah by the Caliph — “the Iron” for persistence in the field. Total 61 — one-dimensional but effective war-king.

  • 17The Iron — his byname is earned; highest martial focus among Bagratids.
  • 12111110Caliphal recognition capped the victory, but otherwise a one-dimensional war king — recovery rather than golden-age building, straightforward warrior politics, and no scholarly epithet.

Gagik Artsruni A tier

Գագիկ Արծրունի

908–943

Why A tier: Founded the Kingdom of Vaspurakan and built the Church of the Holy Cross on Aghtamar — one of the supreme monuments of medieval Armenian architecture. Total 70 — exactly on the A threshold; a complete medieval monarch.

  • 14Broke from Caliphal authority while surviving neighbours.
  • 13Held an independent kingdom by force and treaty.
  • 17Aghtamar and Vaspurakan’s institutions — builder-king.
  • 11Thomas Artsruni chronicles dynasty, not poison.
  • 15Patron of art and architecture at the highest level.

Ashot III Bagratid B tier

953–977

Gagik I Bagratid A tier

Գագիկ Ա Բագրատունի

989–1020

Why A tier: Long, stable reign at the apex of Bagratid Ani — cathedral completed, city past 100,000, great-power diplomacy among Byzantium, the Caliphate, and the Seljuks. Total 74 — the best of the Ani Bagratids on every axis that matters for a medieval king.

  • 16Three decades balancing the powers around Ani.
  • 14Deterrence and defence through strength, not expansion.
  • 17Ani’s golden age is his ledger.

Լևոն Ա Ռուբինյան

1198–1219

Why A tier: First crowned King of Cilician Armenia — recognised by the Holy Roman Empire, Papacy, and Byzantium. Master diplomat among Crusaders, Latins, and Muslims. Total 76 — nearly S-tier; Cilicia’s founding great king.

Հեթում Ա Հեթումյան

1226–1270

Why A tier: Travelled to Karakorum in 1254 and allied Cilicia with the Mongols — shrewd realpolitik that bought decades against the Mamluks. Kirakos Gandzaketsi devotes a long chapter to the journey. Total 72 — Grousset’s “political genius”; diplomacy and intrigue carry him.

  • 13Competent commander; victory came from alliance.
  • 14Kept Cilicia viable under existential pressure.

  • 13Well-travelled, informed ruler.

Levon II Hethumid A tier

Լևոն Բ Հեթումյան

1269–1289

Why A tier: Cilicia’s cultural and economic golden age — patron of Toros Roslin’s manuscript workshop even as Mamluk pressure intensified. Total 70 — exactly on the A line; the last truly flourishing Cilician reign.
