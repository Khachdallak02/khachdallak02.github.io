Artaxias I Artaxiad A tier

Արտաշես Ա Արտաշեսյան

c. 190–160 BC

Why A tier: The founder of the Artaxiad (Արտաշեսյան) dynasty. He expanded Armenia from a small province into a major regional power, established a new capital, and enacted sweeping land reforms. His stewardship and martial achievements are foundational to Armenian history, even if his court lacked the complex intrigue or high culture of later eras.

  • 14After the Seleucid defeat by Rome at Magnesia (Մագնեսիայի ճակատամարտ, 190 BC), Artashes declared independence. He allied with Zariadres (Զարեհ), the ruler of Sophene (Ծոփք), to secure his western flank.

    He maintained a pragmatic peace with the expanding Roman Republic (Հռոմ) and the rising Parthian Empire (Պարթևաստան), avoiding devastating wars with superpowers.
  • 15Rapidly expanded his borders. He conquered Paytakaran (Փայտակարան) from Media, recovered Gugark (Գուգարք) from Iberia, and annexed lands from the Seleucids.

    According to Movses Khorenatsi, he decisively defeated an invasion by the Alans (Ալաններ). At the Battle of the Kur River (Կուր), he allegedly routed an Alan army, capturing their prince and securing the northern frontier.
  • 20A legendary builder and reformer. He founded the new capital of Artaxata (Արտաշատ) on the Ararat plain, allegedly with the advice of the exiled Carthaginian general Hannibal (Հաննիբալ).

    He erected boundary stones (արամեական արձանագրություններով սահմանաքարեր) across the country to regulate private landholding and protect village communities from encroaching nobles.
  • 48A conqueror and administrator, not a schemer. His reign is remembered for open warfare and public reforms rather than court assassinations or shadowy plots. While he established a Hellenistic court, his focus was on state-building, with little record of significant literary or philosophical patronage.

Tigranes II Artaxiad A tier

Տիգրան Բ Արտաշեսյան

95–55 BC

Why A tier: Many Armenians still treat him as the ultimate S-tier king because he built the largest empire in Armenian history. However, that empire collapsed within decades, crushed between Parthia (Պարթևաստան) and Rome (Հռոմ). His martial and stewardship achievements are legendary, but diplomatic miscalculations doomed the empire. He earns a solid A tier for his unmatched peak, even if his legacy was shorter-lived than others.

  • 10His diplomacy was bold but flawed. The Seleucid empire was exhausted by internal dynastic wars. To prevent total ruin, the Syrian (Ասորիք) nobility invited Tigranes to rule them in 84 BC. To secure his borders against Rome (Հռոմ) and Parthia (Պարթևաստան), he allied with Pontus (Պոնտոս).

    He bravely refused the Roman general Lucullus's (Լյուցիոս Լուկուլլոս) demand to surrender the defeated Pontic king. Yet, these moves backfired. The Armenian-Pontic alliance could not defeat the Rome-Parthia axis in the long run. Even his own son, Tigranes the Younger (Տիգրան Կրտսեր), rebelled at a critical moment. Nerve, but no lasting coalitions.
  • 19His battlefield record is nearly flawless. He conquered the Seventy Valleys (Յոթանասուն հովիտներ), seized Media (Մարաստան), and humbled Parthia (Պարթևաստան) to claim the title King of Kings (արքայից արքա).

    He won decisively at Damascus (Դամասկոս) and later shattered the Roman army at Aratsani (Արածանի). He did lose Tigranocerta (Տիգրանակերտ) when Lucullus outflanked him. However, his overall military peak remains unmatched in Armenian history.
  • 18He managed a massive, booming economy. Conquest multiplied Greater Armenia (Մեծ Հայք). He moved his court to Antioch (Անտիոք) to appease the Syrian elites. Realising the empire needed a capital on Armenian soil, he built the magnificent Tigranocerta (Տիգրանակերտ). (I once turned his capital-placement dilemma into a simple graph problem on SPOJ.)

    He controlled trade routes from Phoenicia (Փյունիկիա) to the east. He also boosted the economy by establishing "free cities" (ազատ քաղաքներ) with internal autonomy. He built for scale.
  • 10He successfully managed a vast multi-ethnic court, but was consistently outplayed in the shadows. Rome won the long game through subversion, and the gates of Tigranocerta were opened by traitors from within.
  • 12He brought Hellenistic culture to the elite. Movses Khorenatsi (Մովսես Խորենացի) notes his Greek (հունարեն) education. Plutarch (Պլուտարքոս) describes thriving Greek theatres in Artashat (Արտաշատ) and Tigranocerta.

    The court even tried to merge Armenian and Greek gods, equating Aramazd (Արամազդ) with Zeus (Զևս) and Anahit (Անահիտ) with Artemis (Արտեմիս). However, this was purely a court fashion. The common people rejected it, limiting his cultural impact.

Artavasdes II Artaxiad A tier

Արտավազդ Բ Արտաշեսյան

55–34 BC

Why A tier: A playwright-king who navigated the deadly rivalry between Rome (Հռոմ) and Parthia (Պարթևաստան). He maintained Armenian independence for two decades through shifting alliances, fielding substantial armies, and fostering Hellenistic culture. Exceptional learning and solid diplomacy, dragged down by military caution and his tragic betrayal by Mark Antony.

  • 14Navigated the fallout of the First Triumvirate. After Crassus's defeat in 53 BC, he allied with Parthian king Orodes II (Որոդես Բ), sealing the pact by marrying his sister to the Parthian crown prince Pacorus (Բակուր).

    When Mark Antony (Մարկոս Անտոնիոս) invaded in 36 BC with ~100,000 troops, Artavasdes shifted back to Rome to spare Armenia from destruction.
  • 11Fielded a formidable army, but was highly cautious. During Antony's 36 BC Parthian campaign, Artavasdes provided 6,000 cavalry and 7,000 infantry.

    However, when Antony's siege engines were destroyed at Phraaspa (Հրահատապատ), Artavasdes pragmatically withdrew his forces to preserve them. This earned him Antony's lasting enmity. He avoided pitched battles when possible.
  • 13Maintained the realm's prosperity and borders intact through a highly volatile era. He is not known for founding new cities or major economic reforms like his father Tigranes.
  • 15Survived for 20 years by reading the shifting balance of power between two empires. However, his final miscalculation was fatal.

    In 34 BC, Antony lured him to the Roman camp near Artashat (Արտաշատ) under the guise of discussing a marriage alliance. Artavasdes was arrested, paraded in golden chains in Alexandria (Ալեքսանդրիա), and eventually beheaded by Cleopatra (Կլեոպատրա) in 31 BC.
  • 18Plutarch (Պլուտարքոս) records that Artavasdes composed tragedies, orations, and histories in Greek (հունարեն), some of which survived into the 1st century AD.

    He famously staged Euripides's The Bacchae (Բաքոսուհիներ) at the Armenian court in Artashat, during which the severed head of the Roman general Crassus (Կրասոս) was reportedly used as a stage prop.

Tiridates I Arsacid B tier

Տրդատ Ա Արշակունի

63–88 AD

Why B tier: The founder of the Arsacid (Արշակունի) dynasty in Armenia. He secured his throne through a mix of Parthian military backing and a masterful diplomatic compromise with Rome (Հռոմ). A diplomatic masterpiece culminating in his journey to Rome, though his reign lacked the massive conquests or building projects of the Artaxiads.

  • 16Mastered the art of compromise. The Treaty of Rhandeia (Հռանդեայի պայմանագիր, 64 AD) ended decades of Roman-Parthian war. The agreement was brilliant: Tiridates, a Parthian prince, would rule Armenia, but receive his crown from the Roman Emperor.

    In 66 AD, he travelled overland to Rome with 3,000 retainers. Nero (Ներոն) crowned him in a spectacular ceremony, securing peace for 50 years.
  • 13Fought a grueling war against the Roman general Corbulo (Կորբուլոն). In 58 AD, Corbulo invaded with ~30,000 troops, capturing Artashat (Արտաշատ) and Tigranocerta (Տիգրանակերտ). Tiridates waged a guerrilla war.

    In 62 AD, with Parthian reinforcements from his brother Vologases I (Վաղարշ Ա), Tiridates surrounded and humiliated the Roman army under Paetus at Rhandeia (Հռանդեա), forcing them to surrender and pass under the yoke.
  • 13Rebuilt the capital Artashat (Արտաշատ), which had been burned by Corbulo. Nero provided him with 50 million sesterces and Roman artisans to aid in the reconstruction. He established the Arsacid dynasty that would rule Armenia for nearly four centuries.
  • 12Navigated the complex factional politics of the Parthian court and the Roman Senate. He successfully played the two superpowers against each other to secure his own position without falling victim to internal assassinations.
  • 14Tiridates was a Zoroastrian high priest, or Magus (Մոգ). Pliny the Elder notes that he introduced Magian banquets and rituals to Nero.

    His strict adherence to Zoroastrian purity laws (which forbade him from traveling by sea to avoid defiling the water) dictated his massive, nine-month overland journey to Rome.

Tiridates III Arsacid B tier

Տրդատ Գ Արշակունի

c. 298–330 AD

Why B tier: The king who made Armenia the first Christian state (traditionally 301 AD). He reclaimed his throne with Roman steel and fundamentally transformed Armenian civilization. His civilisational impact is unmatched, but his record reflects a pious, physically imposing warrior-king rather than a universal genius.

  • 14Restored to the throne in 298 AD following the Peace of Nisibis (Մծբինի պայմանագիր) between Rome and Persia. He maintained a strong alliance with Rome (Հռոմ) to protect against Sassanid (Սասանյան) Persia.

    His conversion to Christianity by Gregory the Illuminator (Գրիգոր Լուսավորիչ) aligned Armenia culturally with the Christianizing Roman Empire, permanently separating it from Zoroastrian Persia.
  • 16Famed for his legendary physical strength. Agathangelos (Ագաթանգեղոս) and Movses Khorenatsi (Մովսես Խորենացի) describe him fighting in the Roman army before his restoration, allegedly stopping chariots with his bare hands and defeating Gothic champions in single combat.

    As king, he successfully defended Armenia's borders against Sassanid incursions and northern nomadic raids.
  • 14Executed a massive cultural and institutional overhaul. He destroyed the ancient pagan temples (such as the temple of Anahit in Erez) and transferred their vast estates, wealth, and lands to the newly established Armenian Apostolic Church. This fundamentally restructured the Armenian feudal economy.
  • 11His reign was defined by open religious conflict rather than court scheming. He brutally persecuted Christians (including the martyrdom of the Hripsimian virgins / Հռիփսիմյան կույսեր) before his own conversion. Later, he faced open rebellion from pagan nakharars (նախարարներ) rather than shadowy plots.
  • 13While not a scholar himself, his reign marks the beginning of Armenian Christian literature and historiography. He patronised the early Church, establishing schools to teach Greek and Syriac to the children of pagan priests to prepare them for Christian clergy.

Khosrov II Kotak Arsacid C tier

Խոսրով Բ Կոտակ Արշակունի

330–339 AD

Why C tier: A brief, tragic Arsacid reign caught in the crossfire between Rome and Persia. Roman and Armenian sources are thin and often confuse him with other kings named Khosrov. He was a placeholder king between two giants (Tiridates III and Arshak II), not a builder, conqueror, or scholar.

  • 12His reign was defined by internal plotting and external subversion. The Sassanid king Shapur II (Շապուհ Բ) incited the Armenian nakharars to rebel, leading to constant court chaos. This is the one axis where the sources hint at a story.
  • 12101110Nine years on the throne with almost no independent record in Pavstos Buzand (Փավստոս Բուզանդ) or Movses Khorenatsi (Մովսես Խորենացի). He relied entirely on the veteran sparapet Vache Mamikonian (Վաչե Մամիկոնյան) to fight his wars. His only notable stewardship act was planting the Khosrov Forest (Խոսրովի անտառ) near Dvin as a royal hunting ground.

Arshak II Arsacid C tier

Արշակ Բ Արշակունի

c. 350–368 AD

Why C tier: A defiant king who waged a brutal, decades-long war of survival against Sassanid Persia. He tried to centralise power but alienated his own nobility and the Church. Heroic martial resistance, but his reign ended in diplomatic catastrophe, civil war, and his tragic death in Persian captivity.

  • 16Waged a relentless defensive war against Shapur II (Շապուհ Բ). Pavstos Buzand (Փավստոս Բուզանդ) details numerous battles where Arshak and his legendary sparapet Vasak Mamikonian (Վասակ Մամիկոնյան) repelled massive Persian invasions.

    In one notable engagement, the Armenian cavalry allegedly routed a Persian army of tens of thousands, though exact numbers in Buzand are often stylized. His survival against a superpower defines his martial legacy.
  • 13His court was a viper's nest. He ordered the assassination of his own nephew Gnel (Գնել) and his rebellious cousin Tiridates. His queen, Olympias, was poisoned by his other wife, Pharantzem (Փառանձեմ), who mixed poison into the communion wine.

    Ultimately, Arshak was lured to a "peace conference" by Shapur II, captured, and imprisoned in the Castle of Oblivion (Անհուշ բերդ), where he committed suicide.
  • 101110Diplomatically, he was abandoned by Rome (Հռոմ) after the "Ignoble Peace" (Ամոթալի դաշնագիր) in 363 AD, leaving him to face Persia alone. In stewardship, he tried to break the feudal lords (nakharars) by founding the asylum city of Arshakavan (Արշակավան). The lords retaliated by slaughtering the city's inhabitants (allegedly 20,000 families). He also clashed bitterly with the Church, leaving no scholarly legacy.

Pap Arsacid B tier

Պապ Արշակունի

370–374 AD

Why B tier: A pragmatic and ruthless restorer who clawed back Armenian autonomy after devastating Persian invasions. He curbed the power of the Church and rebuilt the army. Highly effective state-building and intrigue, but his reign was cut short by Roman assassination before he could fully stabilize the realm.

  • 12Reclaimed the throne in 370 AD with the help of the Roman Emperor Valens. However, Pap quickly realized Rome intended to treat Armenia as a vassal rather than an ally.

    He secretly negotiated with Shapur II (Շապուհ Բ) of Persia to secure true independence, playing the two empires against each other until Rome decided he was too dangerous.
  • 13Inherited a ruined kingdom. Alongside sparapet Mushegh Mamikonian (Մուշեղ Մամիկոնյան), he rebuilt the Armenian army to 90,000 men.

    In 371 AD, at the Battle of Bagrevand (Բագրևանդի ճակատամարտ), the combined Armenian-Roman forces decisively crushed a massive Persian invasion force, securing Armenia's borders.
  • 12Enacted radical economic reforms to strengthen the crown. He confiscated vast estates from the Church, closed down numerous monasteries and convents, and forced monks to serve in the army or pay taxes. This replenished the royal treasury and allowed him to rebuild the nation's military and infrastructure.
  • 16A master of ruthless politics. When Catholicos Nerses the Great opposed his reforms, Pap allegedly had him poisoned at a royal banquet. He navigated constant assassination plots from disgruntled nakharars and Roman spies.

    However, he was ultimately outmaneuvered: in 374 AD, the Roman general Trajanus invited him to a feast and had him hacked to death by guards.
  • 9Vilified by Armenian clerical historians (like Pavstos Buzand) for his anti-Church policies and alleged debauchery. He shut down religious institutions and showed no interest in theological or scholarly patronage, earning him the lowest learning score among the major kings.

Vramshapuh Arsacid C tier

Վրամշապուհ Արշակունի

389–417 AD

Why C tier: A peaceful king whose reign is immortalised by a single, monumental civilisational event: the creation of the Armenian alphabet. While he was a mild steward rather than a powerhouse ruler, his patronage secured the survival of Armenian culture through centuries of statelessness.

  • 13Ruled the Persian-controlled eastern half of Armenia after the kingdom was partitioned in 387 AD. He maintained excellent relations with the Sassanid court, securing the release of his brother Khosrov IV (Խոսրով Դ) from the Castle of Oblivion (Անհուշ բերդ). He successfully kept the peace in a divided nation.
  • 9A reign defined by peace and cultural development, not military campaigns. He fought no major wars, focusing entirely on internal stability.
  • 13Funded the massive project of creating a national script. He dispatched scholars to Edessa (Եդեսիա) and Amida (Ամիդ) and established the first Armenian-language schools across the realm, laying the groundwork for a unified national identity.
  • 8A quiet, stable court with no recorded scheming or assassinations. He ruled through consensus with the nakharars (նախարարներ) and the Church.
  • 18His patronage of Mesrop Mashtots (Մեսրոպ Մաշտոց) and Catholicos Sahak Partev (Սահակ Պարթև) led to the invention of the Armenian alphabet in 405 AD.

    According to Koryun (Կորյուն), Vramshapuh actively participated in the intellectual discussions that led to the script's creation. This is arguably the most important cultural event in Armenian history.

Ashot I Bagratid B tier

Աշոտ Ա Բագրատունի

884–890

Why B tier: The indispensable restorer. After centuries of Arab domination, he revived the Armenian crown through careful diplomacy and military persistence. He is the founding father of the Bagratid (Բագրատունի) kingdom, though his relatively short reign as crowned king limits his overall score.

  • 16Achieved a diplomatic masterstroke in 885 AD. He was officially recognized as King of Armenia by both the Abbasid Caliph Al-Mu'tamid (Ալ-Մութամիդ) and the Byzantine Emperor Basil I (Վասիլ Ա Մակեդոնացի).

    He maintained this fragile balance by sending tribute to the Caliphate while forming a strategic alliance with Byzantium, securing international legitimacy for his new kingdom.
  • 14Before his coronation, he spent decades as Prince of Princes (Իշխանաց իշխան), fighting to consolidate power. He defeated rival emirs and subdued rebellious Armenian lords, eventually fielding an army of 40,000 men to secure the borders of his newly independent state.
  • 14Rebuilt the country's infrastructure after the devastating Arab tax policies of the previous centuries. He established his capital at Bagaran (Բագարան) and fostered a period of economic recovery and agricultural growth.
  • 11Navigated the treacherous politics of the Abbasid decline. He used marriage alliances to bind the powerful Artsruni (Արծրունի) and Syuni (Սյունի) families to the Bagratid throne, preventing internal revolts.
  • 12A traditional medieval patron. He supported the Church and monastic centers, which began to flourish again after the restoration of independence, laying the groundwork for the cultural renaissance of later Bagratid kings.

Smbat I Bagratid C tier

Սմբատ Ա Բագրատունի

890–914

Why C tier: A tragic figure known as the Martyr-king. He expanded the realm initially, but his reign ended in a catastrophic war against the Sajid emirs. His gruesome execution—tortured and hung from the walls of Dvin (Դվին)—overshadows his early successes.

  • 13Signed a commercial and political treaty with the Byzantine Empire in 893 AD, angering the Arab emirs. He tried to balance the Caliphate and Byzantium, but the resurgent Sajid dynasty of Azerbaijan viewed his Byzantine tilt as a direct threat.
  • 12Initially successful, defeating an invasion by the Emir of Damascus and expanding Armenian control over Gugark (Գուգարք).

    However, he was ultimately defeated by the Sajid Emir Yusuf (Յուսուֆ). Despite fielding a large army, internal betrayals and Yusuf's superior tactics led to his capture after a year-long siege at the fortress of Kapuyt (Կապույտ բերդ) in 913 AD.
  • 12Moved the capital to Shirakavan (Շիրակավան) and oversaw a period of economic growth before the devastating Sajid invasions ruined the country's infrastructure.
  • 9Completely outmaneuvered by Emir Yusuf, who exploited rivalries among the Armenian nobility. Yusuf famously crowned Gagik Artsruni (Գագիկ Արծրունի) as a rival king in Vaspurakan (Վասպուրական) to divide and conquer the Armenians.
  • 11His reign is best known through the eyewitness accounts of Catholicos Hovhannes Draskhanakerttsi (Հովհաննես Դրասխանակերտցի), who documented the king's tragic end and the suffering of the nation.

Ashot II Bagratid C tier

Աշոտ Բ Բագրատունի

914–929

Why C tier: "The Iron" (Երկաթ) earned his byname through sheer military persistence. He inherited a broken, occupied country and spent his entire reign fighting a brutal counter-offensive to drive out the Arab armies. A highly effective, one-dimensional war king who saved the Bagratid state.

  • 17Waged a relentless guerrilla war against Emir Yusuf (Յուսուֆ) to avenge his father's murder. In 921 AD, at the Battle of Sevan (Սևանի ճակատամարտ), Ashot famously lured the Arab army to the shores of Lake Sevan.

    With only a small force of men hidden in boats, he launched a surprise amphibious assault that routed the much larger Arab army under the commander Beshir. This victory permanently broke the Sajid hold on Armenia.
  • 12111110Travelled to Constantinople in 914 AD to secure Byzantine military aid, returning with a Byzantine auxiliary force. His victories eventually forced the Caliph to recognize him as Shahanshah (Շահնշահ, King of Kings) of Armenia in 922 AD. However, his reign was entirely consumed by warfare and national recovery, leaving no time for the golden-age building or cultural patronage seen in later Bagratid kings.

Gagik Artsruni A tier

Գագիկ Արծրունի

908–943

Why A tier: Founded the Kingdom of Vaspurakan (Վասպուրական) and built the Church of the Holy Cross on Aghtamar — one of the supreme monuments of medieval Armenian architecture. He brilliantly leveraged the conflict between the Bagratids and the Arab emirs to secure his own crown. A complete medieval monarch who defended his borders and left behind a massive cultural legacy.

  • 14Exploited the rivalry between the Bagratid King Smbat I and the Sajid Emir Yusuf. In 908 AD, Yusuf recognized Gagik as King of Armenia to undermine the Bagratids.

    Gagik later reconciled with the Bagratid Ashot II, securing his own kingdom's independence while maintaining a delicate balance of power with the surrounding Arab emirates.
  • 13Successfully defended Vaspurakan from Byzantine and Arab incursions. He maintained a strong standing army that kept his southern borders secure, allowing his kingdom to flourish economically while the rest of Armenia was engulfed in war.
  • 17Transformed Vaspurakan into a wealthy, highly developed state. He built a massive new port city and royal residence on the island of Aghtamar (Աղթամար) in Lake Van, turning it into an impregnable economic hub.
  • 11His initial alliance with Emir Yusuf against his own Armenian overlord (Smbat I) was a ruthless, calculating move that secured his royal title. The historian Thomas Artsruni (Թովմա Արծրունի) chronicles his dynastic maneuvering.
  • 15A patron of art and architecture at the highest level. Between 915 and 921 AD, he commissioned the architect Manuel to build the Cathedral of the Holy Cross (Սուրբ Խաչ եկեղեցի) on Aghtamar.

    The church's intricate exterior bas-reliefs, which include a depiction of Gagik himself presenting the church to Christ, remain a masterpiece of medieval Armenian art.

Ashot III Bagratid B tier

Աշոտ Գ Բագրատունի

953–977

Why B tier: "The Merciful" (Ողորմած). He ushered in a period of peace and prosperity, officially moving the Bagratid capital to Ani (Անի) and transforming it into a major metropolis. An outstanding steward and builder who laid the foundations for Ani's golden age, though he was not a conquering warrior.

  • 15Maintained a fragile peace by balancing the Byzantine Empire to the west and the resurgent Arab emirates to the south. He successfully avoided major wars, allowing the kingdom to recover economically.
  • 12A reign defined by defense rather than expansion. When the Byzantine Emperor John Tzimiskes (Հովհաննես Չմշկիկ) marched a massive army through Armenia in 973 AD, Ashot mobilized an army of 30,000 men to shadow the Byzantines, ensuring they did not annex Armenian territory.
  • 17In 961 AD, he officially transferred the royal capital from Kars to Ani (Անի). He built the city's first line of defensive walls (the "Ashotian walls") and expanded its infrastructure, setting it on the path to becoming the "city of a thousand and one churches."
  • 10A pious and straightforward ruler. He avoided the treacherous internal plotting that plagued other dynasties, ruling through consensus and charity rather than fear.
  • 14Earned his epithet "The Merciful" through his massive patronage of hospitals, leprosariums, and almshouses. He also funded the construction of major monastic complexes, including Sanahin (Սանահին) and Haghpat (Հաղպատ), which became the intellectual centers of medieval Armenia.

Gagik I Bagratid A tier

Գագիկ Ա Բագրատունի

989–1020

Why A tier: The apex of the Bagratid dynasty. Under his long, stable reign, the capital of Ani (Անի) reached its golden age, swelling to a population of over 100,000. He was a master diplomat and a powerful deterrent to foreign invasion, representing the absolute peak of medieval Armenian statehood.

  • 16For three decades, he successfully balanced the aggressive expansion of the Byzantine Empire under Basil II (Վասիլ Բ Բուլղարասպան) with the declining Arab emirates. He maintained peace through a network of strategic marriages and alliances with other Armenian and Georgian princes.
  • 14Maintained a massive standing army of 100,000 men under the command of sparapet Vahram Pahlavuni (Վահրամ Պահլավունի).

    While he rarely went on the offensive, this enormous military force acted as a powerful deterrent. When the Emir of Azerbaijan invaded in 998 AD, Gagik's forces decisively crushed the Arab army at the Battle of Tsumb (Ցումբի ճակատամարտ).
  • 17Ani became one of the largest and wealthiest cities in the world, rivaling Constantinople and Baghdad. He commissioned the architect Trdat (Տրդատ ճարտարապետ) to complete the Cathedral of Ani (Անիի Մայր տաճար) in 1001 AD, a masterpiece that foreshadowed Gothic architecture.
  • 12Kept the notoriously fractious Armenian nobility (nakharars) firmly under control. His court was stable, and he successfully suppressed minor rebellions without allowing them to escalate into civil war.
  • 15A golden age of learning and architecture. His wife, Queen Katranide (Կատրանիդե թագուհի), was a major patron of the Church. The monasteries of Sanahin and Haghpat reached their intellectual peak during his reign, producing scholars and illuminated manuscripts.

Gagik II Bagratid F tier

Գագիկ Բ Բագրատունի

1042–1045

Why F tier: The tragic final king of Bagratid Ani. Crowned as a teenager during a period of intense Byzantine pressure and internal betrayal, he was lured to Constantinople and forced to abdicate. Objectively the weakest reign on the list, ending in the loss of the capital and the dynasty.

  • 8Walked into a fatal trap. In 1044 AD, the Byzantine Emperor Constantine IX Monomachos (Կոստանդին Թ Մոնոմախ) invited the young king to Constantinople under the guise of signing a peace treaty. Despite warnings from his sparapet, Gagik accepted the safe-conduct. He was immediately imprisoned and pressured to surrender his kingdom.
  • 10Before his capture, he and the veteran sparapet Vahram Pahlavuni (Վահրամ Պահլավունի) successfully defended Ani against three separate Byzantine sieges (1042-1043 AD), allegedly routing a Byzantine army of 100,000 men. However, without their king, the city's defenses eventually crumbled.
  • 8Lost the capital. Following his forced abdication in 1045 AD, the gates of Ani were opened to the Byzantines by treacherous nobles. The city would fall to the Seljuk Turks (Սելջուկներ) just two decades later.
  • 7Completely outplayed by the pro-Byzantine faction at his own court, led by the treacherous Catholicos Petros Getadardz (Պետրոս Գետադարձ) and the noble Vest Sarkis (Վեստ Սարգիս), who actively conspired to hand the kingdom over to the Empire.
  • 11Exiled to Cappadocia, he engaged in theological debates with Greek bishops, defending the Armenian Apostolic faith. He was eventually murdered by Byzantine Greeks in 1079 AD.

Levon I Rubenid A tier

Լևոն Ա Ռուբինյան

1198–1219

Why A tier: The first crowned King of Cilician Armenia (Կիլիկիայի հայկական թագավորություն). He transformed a principality into a powerful, internationally recognized kingdom. A master diplomat who navigated the complex politics of the Crusades, the Papacy, and the Holy Roman Empire, laying the foundation for nearly two centuries of Cilician statehood.

  • 18Achieved the ultimate diplomatic goal: international recognition. In 1198 AD, he was crowned King of Armenia in Tarsus (Տարսոն) by the papal legate Conrad of Wittelsbach, receiving royal crowns from both the Holy Roman Emperor Henry VI and the Byzantine Emperor Alexios III Angelos.

    He secured his borders through strategic marriages, marrying his daughters to the kings of Cyprus and the Empire of Nicaea.
  • 15A highly capable commander who expanded Cilicia's borders to the Mediterranean Sea. He successfully defended his realm against the Sultanate of Rum (Իկոնիայի սուլթանություն) and the Ayyubids.

    He captured the strategic fortress of Bagras (Բագրաս) from the Knights Templar and repeatedly intervened in the succession wars of the neighboring Crusader Principality of Antioch (Անտիոք).
  • 16Built a robust, European-style feudal state. He established a royal chancery, minted his own silver and copper coins (դրամ), and granted extensive trading privileges to the Italian maritime republics of Venice and Genoa, turning the port of Ayas (Այաս) into a booming center of international trade.
  • 14Deeply involved in the treacherous politics of the Crusader states. When Bohemond III of Antioch refused to return borrowed funds, Levon lured him to a "banquet" in 1194 AD, captured him, and forced him to sign a treaty highly favorable to Armenia.
  • 13Fostered a unique blend of Armenian and Latin culture at his court. He patronized scholars like Nerses of Lambron (Ներսես Լամբրոնացի) and encouraged the translation of Western legal and medical texts into Armenian.

Hethum I Hethumid A tier

Հեթում Ա Հեթումյան

1226–1270

Why A tier: The architect of the Armenian-Mongol alliance. Recognizing the unstoppable power of the Mongol Empire, he undertook a massive overland journey to Karakorum to secure a treaty that saved Cilicia from destruction. A brilliant political realist who bought his kingdom decades of survival against the Mamluks.

  • 17Executed the most important diplomatic maneuver of the 13th century. In 1254 AD, he traveled thousands of miles across Asia to the court of Möngke Khan (Մանգու խան) in Karakorum.

    He secured a formal alliance that exempted Armenian churches from Mongol taxation and guaranteed Mongol military support against the Seljuks and Mamluks. The historian Kirakos Gandzaketsi (Կիրակոս Գանձակեցի) devotes a detailed chapter to this epic journey.
  • 13Fought alongside his Mongol allies in major campaigns. In 1259 AD, Armenian forces helped the Mongols capture the Syrian cities of Aleppo (Հալեպ) and Damascus (Դամասկոս).

    However, when the Mongols were defeated by the Mamluks at Ain Jalut (Այն Ջալուտ) in 1260 AD, Cilicia was left exposed. In 1266 AD, while Hethum was away seeking Mongol reinforcements, the Mamluks invaded and crushed the Armenian army at the Battle of Mari (Մառիի ճակատամարտ), capturing his son Levon.
  • 14Maintained the economic prosperity of the port of Ayas (Այաս), which became the primary Mediterranean outlet for the Mongol Empire's Silk Road trade. The kingdom grew incredibly wealthy from transit taxes.
  • 15Successfully navigated the deadly, multi-polar politics of the Middle East, balancing the Mongols, the Crusader states, the Byzantine Empire, and the Mamluks. He survived numerous internal plots by rival Armenian barons.
  • 13A well-traveled and highly informed ruler. His court at Sis (Սիս) remained a center of translation and manuscript production, though his personal focus was on statecraft and survival.

Levon II Hethumid A tier

Լևոն Բ Հեթումյան

1269–1289

Why A tier: Presided over the cultural and economic zenith of Cilician Armenia. Despite constant military pressure from the Egyptian Mamluks, his reign saw an explosion of wealth, trade, and the absolute peak of Armenian illuminated manuscript production. The last truly flourishing Cilician king.

  • 15Maintained the crucial alliance with the Mongol Ilkhanate while expanding commercial treaties with the West. He signed highly lucrative trade agreements with the Republic of Genoa and the Catalans, ensuring Western support for his realm.
  • 14Faced relentless invasions from the Mamluk Sultanate (Մամլուքների սուլթանություն). In 1275 and 1276 AD, massive Mamluk armies devastated the Cilician plains. Levon successfully held the mountain fortresses and eventually forced a ten-year peace treaty in 1285 AD, though at the cost of heavy annual tribute.
  • 16Cilicia's economy reached its absolute peak. The port of Ayas (Այաս) became one of the busiest harbors in the Mediterranean, famously visited by Marco Polo in 1271 AD, who described it as a city "good for trade" where merchants from Venice, Genoa, and beyond gathered.
  • 12Maintained internal stability during a time of extreme external stress. He successfully managed the powerful Armenian barons and avoided the disastrous internal civil wars that would plague his successors.
  • 13A massive patron of the arts. His reign coincides with the career of Toros Roslin (Թորոս Ռոսլին), the greatest master of Armenian illuminated manuscripts. The royal scriptoriums at Hromkla (Հռոմկլա) and Sis produced masterpieces of medieval art under his direct sponsorship.

Hethum II Hethumid C tier

Հեթում Բ Հեթումյան

1289–1307

Why C tier: The "Friar-King." A highly educated and deeply pious man who was entirely unsuited for the brutal realities of 13th-century geopolitics. He abdicated the throne three separate times to become a Franciscan monk, plunging the kingdom into dynastic chaos as the Mamluks closed in.

  • 16An erudite scholar and theologian. He was deeply committed to the Catholic Church, taking Franciscan vows and wearing a monk's habit even while ruling. He pushed aggressively for the union of the Armenian and Catholic churches, alienating his own people.
  • 12101011His reign was a political disaster. In 1292 AD, the Mamluks captured Hromkla (Հռոմկլա), the seat of the Armenian Catholicos. Instead of fighting, Hethum repeatedly abdicated in favor of his brothers (Thoros, Smpad, Constantine), only to return to power when they failed or rebelled. This constant "musical chairs" destroyed the kingdom's stability. In 1307 AD, he and his nephew Levon III were assassinated by the Mongol general Bilarghu near Anazarbus (Անարզաբա), ending a chaotic era.

Levon V Lusignan C tier

Լևոն Ե Լուսինյան

1374–1375

Why C tier: The last king of Armenia. A French nobleman of the Lusignan dynasty, he inherited a kingdom that had already been reduced to a single besieged city. He fought a doomed defense with dignity, but the kingdom fell on his watch. He spent the rest of his life as an exiled diplomat in Europe.

  • 13After his capture by the Mamluks, he spent years in a Cairo prison. He was eventually ransomed in 1382 AD through the diplomatic efforts of the King of Castile and the King of Aragon.

    In exile, he traveled between London and Paris, attempting to mediate an end to the Hundred Years' War between England and France, hoping a united Europe would launch a crusade to liberate Armenia.
  • 11Arrived in Cilicia in 1374 AD to find the kingdom reduced to the capital city of Sis (Սիս) and a few mountain forts. He led a desperate, months-long defense of the capital against a massive Mamluk army. Starved and outnumbered, he surrendered the city in April 1375 AD, ending the Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia.
  • 9Inherited total economic collapse. The ports were lost, the trade routes were gone, and the treasury was empty. He had no opportunity to govern or build.
  • 11Survived his imprisonment and exile through his connections to the European nobility, but was unable to outmaneuver the anti-Latin factions among his own Armenian subjects during the siege of Sis.
  • 12A highly cultured European nobleman. He died in Paris in 1393 AD and was granted the ultimate honor of being buried in the Basilica of Saint-Denis (Սեն-Դենի) alongside the Kings of France, where his white marble tomb can still be seen today.
